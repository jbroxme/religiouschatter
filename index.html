Ink and Chatter

Twenty-three figures shared by the Torah, the Bible and the Quran, and by the Book of Mormon, plus five ideas that also reach Hinduism, Buddhism, Scientology and Effective Altruism. Each text pours its own colored ink toward the people it names. Where the inks meet, the stories overlap. Posts that mention them fall like rain. Each text also turns through its own calendar, pulling figures closer when their stories are read or remembered.

        About the numbers. Each count is the number of verses that name the figure. For the Bible I used the Theographic Bible Metadata index of the King James Version, which tells apart different people who share a name. For the Quran I used the word-by-word tagging of the Quranic Arabic Corpus. For the Book of Mormon I used simple wording rules to skip its own Jacob, Joseph, Aaron and Noah. Counts become a rate per 1,000 verses so the long Bible doesn't drown out the shorter texts. "Bible" means everything after the Torah, so its five books aren't counted twice. Satan includes "the devil" in the Bible and Book of Mormon, and Iblis and al-Shaytan in the Quran. Jesus includes "Christ" in the Book of Mormon and al-Masih in the Quran.

        About the roles. Exemplar, tested, transgressor and adversary are a curatorial reading, not a verdict, and they differ by text. David's sin with Bathsheba is in the Bible but not the Quran, where prophets are protected from grave sin. The golden calf is Aaron's work in Exodus and al-Samiri's in the Quran.

        About the calendars. Dates come from your browser's Hebrew and Umm al-Qura Islamic calendars and the Western church year. Jewish and Islamic days begin at sundown, and many Muslim communities fix months by sighting the moon, so local dates can differ by a day. The weekly Torah portion is spread evenly across the year as an approximation; the real schedule doubles some portions. Orthodox churches, which date Easter differently, are not shown yet. Which figures each observance recalls is a curatorial choice.

        About the newer movements. Book of Mormon counts are approximate and count only the same figure: its own Nephite Jacob, Aaron and Ishmael are different people. Scientology's texts and Effective Altruism's books (Singer, MacAskill, Ord) share none of the 23 figures, so they connect through four shared ideas whose weights are a curatorial reading. Effective Altruism is a philanthropic movement, not a religion, and doesn't describe itself as one. Their dials run on the civil year.

        About Hinduism and Buddhism. Neither shares the Abrahamic figures, so they connect through shared ideas and two parallel figures: Manu, who survives a flood like Noah, and Mara, who tempts the Buddha as Satan tempts Jesus. Festival dates are worked out from computed new and full moons in Indian time with simple solar windows. Regional calendars differ, and a leap month can move a festival such as Vesak by a full month.

        About the leaders. A live post counts as a leader's when the account's Bluesky profile describes them as clergy (rabbi, pastor, priest, imam, swami, monk and so on), or when the account is listed in the site's leaders.json file, which is where official accounts go. Profiles are self-described and unverified. Simulated leader posts are written in the voice of a role, never a real person.

        About the chatter. On the website, posts come live from Bluesky's public Jetstream feed. Your browser reads every new post and keeps the ones that name a figure, an idea or a text, in English, Hebrew, Arabic or a few European languages. Common names like David, Mary or Jacob only count with a religious word nearby. Posts with adult or abusive content are skipped, but no filter is perfect: turn off "Show the text of live posts" to show only the drops. Where the live feed can't connect, as in this preview, the posts are simulated.
